# Paddy harvest animation

The homepage opens with a full-screen harvest animation, the published Farminix logo at the top, and the tagline **Family Choice Brand**. The scene uses a deep purple background, a light purple logo without a surrounding card, and the actual Farminix Family Choice Rice bag beneath the falling grains. The footer uses the same light purple logo treatment; the regular site header retains its original brand colors. The opening autoplays a complete ten-second harvest sequence before revealing the homepage. Completion comes from the animation itself, rather than a wall-clock dismissal timer. There is no Skip / Enter button or Escape shortcut to cut the sequence short. Pausing or switching away from the tab preserves the remaining animation; returning resumes it. It replays on a page reload but does not replay during internal navigation. The existing intro enabled setting controls it; the configured story videos remain available through Watch Our Story.

The same scene remains immediately below the main hero and above the product collection. Desktop uses native WebGL 2 with instanced geometry for paddy stems, narrow leaves, grain clusters, and individual falling grains. Phones and touch devices use a lightweight SVG animation with shaded grains, swaying paddy plants, and continuously falling rice; playback requires no video permission, GPU context, or tap. The attached paddy photograph is preserved as `public/paddy-harvest-reference.png` and used as a faint monochrome texture over the purple backdrop. The existing transparent `public/farminix_rice_front.png` is reused without modifying the product packaging.

Grains detach from their moving stem positions, accelerate downward, rotate around multiple axes, and move through perspective depth. Warm lighting gives the grains their rounded surface. The paddy sways gently; cursor movement changes the camera perspective.

Implementation:

- `src/components/PaddyHarvestScene.tsx`: controls, visibility handling, reduced-motion preferences, and fallback.
- `src/components/paddyHarvestRenderer.ts`: geometry, lighting, wind, grain release, gravity, and renderer cleanup.
- `src/components/PaddyHarvestFallback.tsx`: mobile and unsupported-WebGL paddy plants and shaded grains.
- `src/components/harvestTiming.ts`: full opening-sequence and falling-grain durations shared by the desktop and mobile renderers.
- `src/components/PaddyHarvestScene.css`: responsive layout, purple atmosphere, product placement, and vector animation.
- `src/components/IntroVideoOverlay.tsx` and its CSS: opening screen, logo, timing, and entry controls. A native modal dialog manages keyboard focus and prevents interacting with the page underneath; page scrolling is restored on close.
- `src/App.tsx`: lazy loading with the enabled homepage hero section.

The animation can be paused and resumed. It pauses when its visual stage is offscreen or when the document is hidden. Startup checks visibility directly after mounting and on scroll, resize, page return, and visibility changes, rather than depending solely on the intersection observer. Rendering is initialized only when the stage is visible; the below-hero scene stays suspended while the intro is open. The opening waits for the logo and product image to load (or report an image error), then plays automatically, including when reduced motion is enabled, as explicitly requested. Harvest CSS explicitly preserves its real animation durations against the storefront's global reduced-motion rule, which otherwise reduces every CSS animation to 0.01 ms and prematurely ends the intro. Reduced motion uses the simpler vector renderer without stalk sway; the inline homepage scene still starts paused with a visible Play control. Desktop caps render pixel density and releases GPU resources on unmount. Browsers without WebGL 2 or that lose their graphics context automatically receive the complete animated vector scene. Mobile completes after all staggered finite grain animations, while desktop completes after its renderer has actually played the entire sequence; both leave the bag visible before entry.

Validation covered real WebGL rendering, moving and falling grains, pause/resume, offscreen suspension, mobile layout, reduced-motion preferences, and the unsupported-WebGL fallback. A short MP4 preview was recorded separately; the website runs the interactive renderer directly and does not download that video.
